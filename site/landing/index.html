Security

Built for the moment you sign.

Bad signatures are where people get hurt, so most of the design goes there.

Plain-English signing

Before your wallet opens, Stillwater says what the signature allows, like “This lets Stillwater move up to 250 CMD. Nothing else.”

Exact-amount approvals

Approvals default to the amount you are swapping. Unlimited is there for power users, with the trade-off stated.

Approvals manager

See every app that can spend your tokens, flag the risky ones, and revoke without leaving the app.

Impostor-token warnings

Verified badges, contract addresses you can copy, and a clear warning for new or low-liquidity tokens.

FAQ

Questions, answered plainly.

Can I use Stillwater with real funds?

Not yet. This is a demo build designed by Olayemi. The tokens, prices, yields and addresses you see are example data that show how Stillwater works.

What does “you receive at least” mean?

It is the smallest amount your swap can return. If the market moves so you would get less, the swap cancels instead, and only the network fee is spent.

Why approve before I swap?

Your wallet has to allow an app to move a token before the swap can use it. Stillwater asks for the exact amount you are swapping, not your whole balance, and explains it once.

What is slippage, in plain words?

How far the price may move against you while your swap is on its way. Stillwater shows it as money, like “at most $1.25”, so the setting means something.

How is staking risk shown?

Every pool has a four-line risk label: lock-up, withdrawal time, what can go wrong, and price. Low, medium and high badges use the only colours on the page.

What happens if a transaction fails?

You see what happened in plain words, how much gas was spent, and a suggested fix with a one-tap retry. Stuck transactions can be sped up or cancelled, with the cost of each.
